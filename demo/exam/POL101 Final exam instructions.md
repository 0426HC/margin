# POL101: Founding Arguments. Final exam instructions

This is a fictional exam sheet, written for Margin's demo course. It is not from a real course.

**Date:** December 14
**Length:** 2 hours
**Total:** 100 marks

The exam covers Federalist Nos. 1, 10 and 51 (weeks 1 to 3). Bring a pen. No notes or books.

## Part I: Terms (50 marks)

Identify and explain the significance of **5 of the following 8 terms**. Write a short paragraph for each: say what the term means and why it matters to the argument of the paper it comes from. 10 marks each.

1. Faction
2. The large republic
3. Publius
4. Ambition must be made to counteract ambition
5. Separation of powers
6. Majority tyranny
7. Refining and enlarging the public views
8. Auxiliary precautions

## Part II: Essay (50 marks)

Write **one essay**, choosing **one** of the two questions below.

A. Madison says the causes of faction cannot be removed, only its effects controlled. Explain his argument in Federalist No. 10 and assess whether the large republic is a convincing remedy.

B. "If men were angels, no government would be necessary." Using Federalist No. 51, explain how the Constitution is meant to keep government in check without relying on the virtue of those who run it.

## Marking guide

**A term, full marks (10):** a correct, precise definition in your own words; the paper it comes from; and one clear sentence on its significance, meaning how it moves Publius's argument forward. A definition alone earns at most 5.

**The essay, full marks (50):** a clear thesis that answers the question asked; accurate use of the papers with specific references; an explanation of the reasoning, not a summary of it; at least one weakness or objection considered and answered; and a conclusion that links back to the thesis. Essays that only retell the paper earn at most 25.
